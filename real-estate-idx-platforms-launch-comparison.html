Executive summary

The price of IDX isn't the price of a full client platform.

A low-cost IDX subscription provides the listing display and search experience. RealScout is more expensive because it adds mature buyer/seller engagement and automated nurturing. We can build those workflows ourselves, but it takes deliberate product design, ongoing operation, and MLS-compliant data handling.

What the numbers mean

The cheapest monthly recurring option isn't necessarily the best system—and the lowest operating cost may not justify building an IDX from scratch if a plugin already handles that layer.

At two agents
$87 vs. $299

IDX Broker Core ($87 including the published MLS PIN data fee) versus RealScout Build ($299). But they are not feature-equivalent: RealScout's lead-nurturing and collaboration features are much more mature.

Direct MLS access vs. vendor service
$100 brokerage / $525 vendor

MLS PIN publishes different monthly fees for brokerage and third-party vendor access. Neither fee automatically permits a multi-brokerage white-label SaaS offering.

Avoid duplicate charges
IDX subscription ≠ raw MLS feed

When we subscribe to a provider's approved IDX service, we normally pay its package and applicable pass-through fees. We don't also need the direct $100 feed simply to display that vendor's search on the brokerage site.

What we're actually building

Make nurture and search separate, reusable systems.

The platform should keep its own CRM, agent permissions, automation and local SEO content regardless of which IDX feed ultimately powers listing display.

01 · Own

Website, SEO, AEO & GEO

Custom neighborhood pages, relocation guides, agent bios, structured data, optimized listings where authorized, FAQs, analytics, and a strong local content program. IDX alone doesn't create a winning local SEO strategy.

02 · Own

CRM and lead nurturing

Single lead record, sources and attribution, owner assignment, consent-based email/text workflows, property interests, appointment tracking, seller follow-up and role-based reporting.

03 · License or build

IDX listing-data layer

Initially consume an approved IDX plugin or—after explicit MLS authorization—build a direct RESO feed adapter. Keep the listing data and its licensing boundaries separate from CRM data.

Before signing anything

Eight questions to ask every vendor

  1. 1. Exact two-agent totalDoes this quote include both owners, hosting, IDX, setup, MLS fees, contact limits and automation?
  2. 2. MLS PIN approvalAre we approved for MLS Property Information Network, and how long does onboarding actually take?
  3. 3. Listing SEODo property detail pages live on our domain and render crawlable content? What does the HTML look like?
  4. 4. API and webhooksCan we send events to our own WordPress CRM? Which data is exportable or restricted?
  5. 5. CRM and lead exportsCan we export contacts, tags, notes, activity, campaign consent and attribution if we switch vendors?
  6. 6. Agent growthHow do prices change at 5, 10 and 25 agents? Is a new website billed separately for each agent?
  7. 7. Nurturing and messagingAre saved-search alerts, seller reports, behavior triggers, bulk email and text included? Any volume limits?
  8. 8. Exit and ownershipWho owns the domain, page content, property URLs, designs, data, integrations and automations at cancellation?
